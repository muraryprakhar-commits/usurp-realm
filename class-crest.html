Where the class crest comes from

The idea of a crest that belongs to one graduating class, rather than to the whole school, has a long history in the United States. According to the U.S. Military Academy at West Point, it was the first school to use rings as a class symbol, starting with the Class of 1835. Since the Class of 1917, each West Point ring has carried the academy's crest on one side and the class's own crest on the other, and every class since 1835 has chosen its own motto to be part of that crest. The first class picked "Danger Brings Forth Friendship".

That split is a useful way to think about any class crest. The school already has its emblem. The class crest is about the people who went through it together: the year, the friendships and a line that sums up how it felt.

Designing a crest your whole class will wear

Put the year where everyone sees it

The class year is the one thing every member shares, so make it the name or the motto. "Class of 2027" across the top is clear and lasts; a school name plus "Seniors 2027" works too. Names in Crest Studio can be up to 40 characters, and mottos up to 48.

Choose an emblem for what the class is about

  • Laurel: the old symbol of victory and achievement, a natural fit for finishing school.
  • Star: heralds call it a mullet. It suits a class that aims high, and pairs well with the Latin motto Per aspera ad astra ("through hardships to the stars").
  • Flame or book: learning and ideas, good for a whole graduating class or an honor society.
  • Mountain or compass: the road ahead, for a class heading off in every direction.
  • Key: a class that is opening the next door, and a nice choice for a reunion crest.
  • Your mascot's cousin: if the school team is the Lions, Eagles, Wolves or Bears, the matching beast gives the crest an instant connection. See the lion coat of arms guide for poses.

Colors that print well

Start from the school colors and pair a color (red, blue, green, purple or black) with gold or silver for the emblem. That is heraldry's rule of tincture, a metal on a color, and it also keeps the crest readable on a dark hoodie or a yearbook page. If the school colors are two colors with no metal, use one as the field and gold or silver for the emblem.

A motto that sounds like your class

Latin gives a crest a formal ring: Fiat lux ("let there be light") and Per aspera ad astra are both long-used Latin mottos, and the Latin motto generator has more with translations. An English motto with a class in-joke is often the one people remember at the ten-year reunion.

Make your own, do not borrow

School and university crests and mascots usually belong to the school, and many are trademarks. Design an original class crest with your own year, colors and emblem, and if the crest will go on official school merchandise or in the yearbook, check with your adviser about the school's brand rules first.

Questions

What is a class crest?

A crest that belongs to one graduating class rather than the whole school, usually with the class year and a motto chosen by the class. West Point has paired a class crest with the academy crest on its class rings since the Class of 1917.

Can we use our school logo or mascot on the class crest?

Only with the school's permission, because school crests and mascots usually belong to the school and many are trademarks. An original crest with your class year, school colors and a related emblem avoids the problem.

Will the crest print well in the yearbook?

Yes. Yearbook printers generally ask for 300 dots per inch at print size. The 4000 by 5000 px print file is about 13 by 16 inches at 300 dpi, and the SVG scales to any size.

Can we print it on class shirts?

Yes. Once bought, you can use the files for any lawful purpose, including shirts, hoodies and banners. The SVG is the file most printers want.
