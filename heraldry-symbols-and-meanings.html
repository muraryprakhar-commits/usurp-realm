Heraldry Symbols and Meanings

What the colors, divisions and emblems on a coat of arms traditionally mean, and how to use them to design a crest that says something true about you.

Read the guideDesign a crest
A word of honesty. Early heralds chose colors and shapes to be recognized, not to send a message. The meanings below were written down later, mostly by heraldic authors of the 1500s and 1600s, and different books disagree. Use them as inspiration, not rules.

Heraldic colors (tinctures)

Heraldry calls its colors tinctures. There are two metals, five main colors and a few rarer "stains".

The rule of tincture

Never put a metal on a metal or a color on a color. Gold on blue reads from across a field; red on blue blurs. Every example on this site follows the rule, and following it is the single easiest way to make a crest look authentic.

Divisions and ordinaries

The broad shapes that divide or cross the shield. Heralds call bands like the chevron and fess "ordinaries" and splits like per pale "partitions".

Emblems (charges)

Anything placed on the shield is a charge. These are the emblems available in Crest Studio. Cadency marks, mentioned below, were small symbols English heralds used to tell sons of the same father apart.

Put it together

  1. Pick a metal and a color whose meanings fit you (say, Azure for loyalty and Or for generosity).
  2. Choose a division with a story (a chevron for a family home, per pale to join two families).
  3. Add one emblem (a key for the family that keeps everyone together).
  4. Finish with a motto from the Latin motto generator or your own words.

Questions

Did medieval knights choose colors for their meaning?

Mostly no. Early heraldry was about being recognizable. The symbolic meanings of colors and charges were written down later, largely by heraldic authors in the 1500s and 1600s, and they vary from book to book. They are a fine source of inspiration for a modern crest.

What is the rule of tincture?

Never put a metal on a metal or a color on a color. Gold and silver are metals; red, blue, green, purple and black are colors. Gold on blue is correct; red on blue breaks the rule and is hard to read at a distance.

What does a crescent mean on a coat of arms?

It is often said to stand for hope of greater glory. In English heraldry a small crescent is also the cadency mark of a second son.

What does a star (mullet) mean in heraldry?

A mullet is a five-pointed star, originally the rowel of a spur. It is often read as a sign of a person who shines or of divine favor, and in English cadency it marks a third son.
