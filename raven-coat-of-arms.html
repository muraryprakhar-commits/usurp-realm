What a raven means on a coat of arms

Wisdom, memory and foresight. Ravens are among the cleverest birds alive, and people seem to have noticed that for a very long time. In Norse myth the god Odin keeps two ravens, Huginn and Muninn, whose names are usually translated as "thought" (or "mind") and "memory". The Prose Edda says they sit on his shoulders and fly out over the world each day, then come back and tell him what they have seen. In the Poetic Edda, Odin says he fears that Huginn may not come back, but fears even more for Muninn.

So a raven on a shield says something different from a lion or an eagle. It is not about strength. It is about paying attention, knowing what is going on and remembering who your people are. That makes it a natural emblem for a family that keeps its stories, a team that wins by outthinking the other side, or a writer's invented house that always seems to know more than it says.

Raven, crow or corbie?

Heraldry does not usually tell ravens, crows and rooks apart. They are all drawn as the same black bird, and older English and Scots writers often call it a corbie. By default it is shown close, standing with its wings folded, which is how the raven in Crest Studio is drawn: side-on, standing, with a strong beak and a long tail.

The one cousin heralds do keep separate is the Cornish chough, which looks similar but has a red beak and red legs. Three choughs appear on the arms of the city of Canterbury, so if you see black birds with red feet on a shield, they are choughs, not ravens.

Famous ravens in history and heraldry

The Viking raven banner

Ravens were flown on Viking war flags. The Anglo-Saxon Chronicle records that in 878, when a Viking force was defeated in Devon, "there was taken the war-flag which they called the Raven". It is one of the earliest written mentions of a raven used as an emblem in Britain, centuries before heraldry proper began.

The Corbet family

One of the best-known raven coats of arms in England belongs to the Corbet family of Shropshire, whose lands included the barony of Caus and the village of Moreton Corbet. Their name comes from an Anglo-Norman word for a crow or raven, and their arms make the pun plain: black ravens on gold. A seal of 1301 shows two ravens, and later generations of the family used a single raven. Heralds call this kind of pun canting arms. The family motto, Deus pascit corvos, means "God feeds the ravens".

The raven king of Hungary

Matthias Corvinus, King of Hungary from 1458 to 1490, took his byname from the Latin corvus, raven, and the raven was the emblem of his family, the Hunyadis. It is usually shown holding a gold ring in its beak. A popular legend says a raven once stole a gold ring from the young Matthias (in another version, from his father, John Hunyadi). His raven still appears in Budapest, including at Matthias Church in Buda Castle. He even named his famous library, the Bibliotheca Corviniana, after the bird.

Poland and the Tower of London

In Poland, the raven appears in the clan arms called Ślepowron, shared by many families. The Polish coat of arms guide explains how one herb can belong to hundreds of surnames. And in London, at least six ravens are kept at the Tower, with a legend that the kingdom will fall if they leave. The legend sounds ancient, but the earliest known picture of ravens at the Tower dates from 1883, and the first written version of the "kingdom will fall" story comes from 1944. A good reminder that heraldic stories are worth checking.

These arms belong to their families, cities and countries, so treat them as inspiration and give your own raven its own colors, name and motto.

Designing your raven crest

Colors

A black raven on gold is the classic, the colors of the Corbets, and it looks strong in print and on screens. A black raven on silver is cooler and starker. For a night-sky look, flip it: a silver raven on black. Silver on purple or gold on blue suit a gaming team or a bookshop. Each pair keeps the rule of tincture, a metal (gold or silver) against a color. The heraldry meanings guide explains what each color traditionally stands for.

Keep the field simple

A raven is a dark, compact shape, so it reads best on a plain field. If you add a division, make sure the raven sits on a metal, or switch it to silver so it does not disappear into a dark half of the shield.

Canting arms for raven names

If your surname has a raven or crow in it, such as Ravenscroft, Ravenhill, Corbin, Crowley or Crowe, a raven is the obvious pun, and the oldest heraldic joke there is. The owl coat of arms guide tells the story of another bird pun, the owls of Oldham.

Teams, clans and invented worlds

Ravens are a favorite for gaming clans, quiz teams and anyone with a Norse theme, and the silhouette holds up well as a small avatar. Writers often give a raven to a house of spies, scholars or keepers of secrets: see fantasy heraldry for writers. Mottos

Try plain English such as "Thought and Memory", "Watch and Remember" or "We Forget Nothing", or Latin like Sapere Aude (dare to be wise) or Veritas Vincit (truth conquers). The Latin motto generator has more, each with a translation.

Questions

What does a raven mean on a coat of arms?

Wisdom, memory and foresight. The link goes back at least to Norse myth, where the god Odin has two ravens, Huginn and Muninn, usually translated as thought and memory, who fly over the world and bring him news.

Is there a difference between a raven and a crow in heraldry?

Usually not. Heraldry draws ravens, crows and rooks as the same black bird, often called a corbie. The Cornish chough is the exception: it is shown with a red beak and red legs.

Which families have a raven on their coat of arms?

A well-known English example is the Corbet family of Shropshire, whose arms show black ravens on gold as a pun on their name. In Hungary the raven was the emblem of the Hunyadi family and of King Matthias Corvinus, and in Poland it appears in the Ślepowron clan arms.

Is a raven a good emblem for a gaming clan or team?

Yes. It suggests intelligence and a sharp eye rather than brute strength, and its dark, compact silhouette stays readable at small sizes such as a Discord icon.

Can I put a raven on my family crest?

Yes. The raven is a traditional heraldic charge anyone can use in an original design. Avoid copying a real family or civic coat of arms exactly, and treat a Crest Studio design as decorative art rather than official arms.
