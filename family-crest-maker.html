Is there a "real" crest for my surname?

Probably not in the way websites selling surname crests suggest. In English and Scottish heraldry a coat of arms is granted to one person and inherited by their descendants. It never belonged to everyone with the same last name. So the "Smith coat of arms" printed on a mug belongs, at most, to one Smith family line that may have nothing to do with yours.

That is good news. You are free to design a crest from scratch that says something true about your own family, and that is what heralds did for every new family in the first place.

Crest or coat of arms? Strictly, the crest is the small figure on top of the helmet and the coat of arms is the shield. Most people say "family crest" for the whole design, and so do we.

How to design a family crest with meaning

1. Pick two colors that say something about you

Heraldic colors carry traditional meanings: gold for generosity, blue for loyalty, green for hope, red for courage, purple for justice. Choose one for the field and one for the division. Pair a metal (gold or silver) with a color (red, blue, green, purple or black) so it reads clearly from across a room. See what each color and symbol means.

2. Choose a division

A chevron is often read as the roof of a home, which suits a family crest. Per pale splits the shield in two, the traditional way to join two families. Quarterly gives you four parts of one story.

3. Choose an emblem

A tower for a family that built a home and protects it. A key for trust and keeping what matters safe. A rose for love. A star for a family that aims high. A crescent for hope. Or simply your family initial.

4. Write a family motto

The best mottos are something your family already says. If you would like Latin, our Latin motto generator has more than 50 established mottos with translations. Short mottos (under about 25 letters) print largest on the ribbon.

5. Keep it simple

Medieval arms were designed to be recognized on a battlefield. One strong emblem and two contrasting colors will look better on a mug, a banner or an embroidered patch than a busy design.

Questions

Is there a real coat of arms for my surname?

In English and Scottish heraldry, arms belong to one person and pass to their descendants. They do not belong to everyone who shares a surname, so a "surname crest" sold online is at best the arms of one particular family line. Designing your own is the honest, personal option.

What is the difference between a crest and a coat of arms?

Strictly, the crest is the small figure that sits on top of the helmet above the shield, and the coat of arms is the design on the shield. In everyday speech most people say "family crest" for the whole thing, and that is fine.

Can I combine two family names?

Yes. Use a name like "House Rossi-Chen" and the Per pale division, which splits the shield into two halves. Joining two families side by side on one shield is an old heraldic custom called impalement.

Can I put it on T-shirts, mugs or a reunion banner?

Yes. Once you buy the files you can use them for any lawful purpose, including printing and merchandise. The SVG scales to any size without blurring.

Is this an official grant of arms?

No. It is original decorative artwork in the style of heraldry. Official grants come from heraldic authorities such as the College of Arms in England or the Court of the Lord Lyon in Scotland.
