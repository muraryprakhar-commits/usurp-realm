English mottos have a long history

Mottos are usually thought to have grown out of medieval war cries and rallying calls, the words a lord's followers shouted to find each other in battle. Over time they turned into short statements of what a family hoped for, believed in or wanted to be remembered for, written on a scroll with the coat of arms.

Latin is the language most people picture, but it was never the only one. Mottos appear in French, English, Scots, Welsh, Irish and many other languages. In the large online catalog of heraldic mottos kept by Heraldry of the World, English is the second most common language after Latin. Modern institutions use English mottos too: the coat of arms of British Airways carries the motto To Fly, To Serve.

English mottos have one big advantage: everyone who sees your crest knows what it says. Nobody has to ask for a translation at the family reunion.

How to write your own family motto

Start with a sentence you already say

The best mottos are often hiding in plain sight: the thing a grandparent always said, the line on the fridge, the words you use when someone in the family is having a hard week. Write down three or four of those, then cut each one to its core.

Keep it to two to six words

A motto sits on a ribbon under the shield, so it has to read at the size of a framed print and of a profile picture. Short wins. Crest Studio allows up to 48 characters, but most of the mottos above are under 30, and they look better for it.

Use rhythm

Pairs and contrasts stick in the memory: "Bold in Heart, Kind in Hand", "Slow Roots, Strong Oaks". Read it aloud. If it sounds like something you could say at a toast, it is working.

Decide how long it should last

A crest for a wedding or a family tree will be looked at for decades, so a timeless line usually serves it best. A crest for a pet, a fantasy league or a group chat can be as silly as you like. The pet coat of arms guide has more ideas in that spirit.

Match the emblem

A motto and an emblem that echo each other make the whole design feel deliberate: a key with an open-door motto, an acorn or oak with a growth motto, a book with a learning motto. The symbols and meanings guide explains what each color and emblem has traditionally stood for.

English or Latin?

Choose Latin if you want the classic look and do not mind explaining it. Choose English if you want the words to land at once, or if the motto is a family saying that would lose its flavor in translation. Some families split the difference: a Latin motto on the crest and its English meaning on the certificate.

If Latin appeals, the Latin motto generator lists more than 50 established Latin phrases with their translations and sources, so you are not relying on a guessed translation.

Can you change it later? In English heraldry a motto is not part of the grant of arms, and holders of arms may adopt or change it as they wish. Scottish heraldry is stricter: the motto is recorded with the arms and usually sits on a scroll above the crest. Questions

What is a good family motto?

A good family motto is short (two to six words), sounds natural when read aloud and says something true about the family: a value, a habit or a hope. Phrases a family already says to each other often make the best mottos.

Does a family motto have to be in Latin?

No. Latin is the most common language for heraldic mottos, but English, French, Scots, Welsh, Irish and many other languages are used too. An English motto has the advantage that everyone can read it.

Can I change our family motto?

In English heraldry, mottos are not part of the grant of arms and can be adopted or changed at will. In Scotland the motto is recorded with the arms, so changing it means rematriculating the arms with the Lord Lyon. For an original design made in Crest Studio, you can change the motto whenever you like.

Where does the motto go on a coat of arms?

In English heraldry it usually sits on a scroll below the shield. In Scottish heraldry it usually sits on a scroll above the crest. In Crest Studio the motto goes on the ribbon below the shield.

Are these mottos historical family mottos?

No. They are suggestions written for this page as starting points, not copied from any family's arms. Short phrases like these may also have been used by others, which is normal for mottos.
