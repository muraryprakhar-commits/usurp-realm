Hnefatafl Rules: How to Play Viking Chess

A king, his guard and a ring of raiders. The two sides are unequal and want different things, which is what makes Viking chess unlike any game you know. These are the rules our Brandubh and Tablut games use.

One family, many rule sets

Hnefatafl is the best known of the tafl games, played across the Viking world. Nobody wrote down the full Viking rules, so every modern rule set is a reconstruction, and they differ in the details: whether the king escapes to the edge or a corner, whether he can capture, how many raiders it takes to catch him. This guide covers exactly one set, the one our game plays, so what you read here is what happens on the board.

The two boards

  • Brandubh, 7 by 7: 8 raiders against the king and 4 defenders. A quick game, and the best place to start.
  • Tablut, 9 by 9: 16 raiders against the king and 8 defenders. Tablut was recorded by Carl Linnaeus in 1732, on his expedition to Lapland.

On both boards the king starts on the throne, the center square, with his defenders around him. The raiders start in groups at the middle of each edge. The four corners and the throne are special squares.

Moving

The raiders move first. Every piece, the king included, moves like a rook in chess: any number of empty squares in a straight line, never diagonally and never jumping over another piece.

Only the king may stop on the throne or on a corner. Other pieces may not stop on the throne or pass through it.

Capturing

You take an enemy piece by trapping it between two of your own pieces in a straight line, across the board or down it. Pieces are taken only by the move that closes the trap, so a piece may safely move into the gap between two enemies.

  • A corner counts as one of your pieces when you trap against it. So does the throne, but only while it is empty.
  • The king fights. He can be one of the two pieces in a defenders' capture.
  • One move can take more than one piece, if it closes traps on several sides at once.

Catching the king

The king is harder to take than other pieces, and how hard depends on where he stands:

  • On the throne: raiders on all four sides.
  • Next to the throne: raiders on the other three sides. The throne counts as the fourth.
  • Anywhere else: two raiders on opposite sides, like any other piece. A corner can stand in for one of them.

The edge of the board does not help the raiders: a king with his back to the edge cannot be trapped against it.

Winning, and draws

  • The defenders win the moment the king reaches any of the four corners.
  • The raiders win by capturing the king.
  • A side that has no legal move on its turn loses.
  • The game is drawn if the same position comes round three times, or if 50 moves each pass without a capture.

Your first games

As the king, open two routes to corners at the same time: the raiders can block only one per move. As the raiders, guard the squares next to the corners before you go after the king, because a king who reaches the edge near an open corner is usually gone.
