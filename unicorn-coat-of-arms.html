What a unicorn means in heraldry

Purity, courage and a strength that cannot be tamed. In medieval stories the unicorn was fierce and impossible to capture by force, so it came to stand for something noble and free. Heraldic meanings come from later tradition rather than strict rules, but the unicorn's has stayed remarkably steady for five hundred years.

The heraldic unicorn is not quite a horse with a horn. Heralds traditionally draw it with a goat's beard, cloven hooves and a lion's tail, which is why it looks wilder and more ancient than the unicorns of modern cartoons.

Scotland and the unicorn

The unicorn is Scotland's national animal, and its place in Scottish royal heraldry goes back centuries. James I of Scotland, who reigned from 1406 to 1437, used a unicorn, and from James V onward a pair of unicorns supported the royal arms. Scotland even had a gold coin called the unicorn, first issued under James III and in circulation from 1484 to 1525.

The royal unicorns are shown crowned and wearing gold chains. A popular explanation is that only a Scottish king was strong enough to tame such a wild creature, so the chain shows power held in check rather than a prisoner.

The lion and the unicorn

In 1603 James VI of Scotland also became James I of England and Ireland. His royal arms put the English lion on one side of the shield and the Scottish unicorn on the other, and that pairing still supports the royal arms of the United Kingdom today. In the version used in Scotland, the unicorn takes the place of honor on the left as you look at it.

Designing your unicorn crest

Colors

A silver unicorn on blue is the most Scottish look, echoing the saltire flag's white on blue. Silver on red is bright and dramatic, gold on green feels like an old forest tale, and silver on purple or black looks elegant and a little mysterious. All keep the rule of tincture: a metal against a color.

Questions

What does a unicorn mean on a coat of arms?

Purity, courage and a strength that cannot be tamed. In medieval stories the unicorn could not be captured by force, so it became a symbol of nobility and freedom.

Why is the Scottish unicorn chained?

The royal unicorns of Scotland are shown crowned and chained. The usual explanation is that only a Scottish king could tame such a wild creature, so the chain represents controlled power.

Why are there a lion and a unicorn on the royal coat of arms?

When James VI of Scotland also became King of England and Ireland in 1603, his arms paired the English lion with the Scottish unicorn as supporters. The pairing remains on the royal arms of the United Kingdom.

Is the unicorn the national animal of Scotland?

Yes. The unicorn has been a Scottish royal emblem for centuries and is widely recognized as Scotland's national animal.

Can I put a unicorn on my family crest?

Yes. The unicorn is a traditional heraldic creature open to anyone in an original design, either on the shield or as a pair of supporters. Just avoid copying the royal arms exactly.
