Designing a crest logo that works

Make it readable at 32 pixels

A logo spends most of its life small: a browser tab, a map pin, a social avatar. Pick one bold emblem and two strongly contrasting colors. Heraldry's own rule helps here: put a metal (gold or silver) against a color (red, blue, green, purple or black). The kit's shield-only files exist for the smallest sizes.

Let the emblem hint at what you do

A sheaf of wheat for a brewery or bakery, a key for an inn or a locksmith, a crescent or sun for a coffee shop that opens early, a lion, eagle or raven for a team, an open book for a bookshop, crossed swords for a barbershop, scales for a law firm. There are 50 emblems in the studio. Or use your initial, which never goes out of date. The heraldry meanings guide explains the traditional symbolism of each.

Keep the name short

Two or three words sit at full size across the top of the crest. Put a longer tagline in the motto ribbon instead.

Before you print a thousand cups: a tool-made design is not exclusive, because someone else can make something similar. If the logo matters to your business, search existing trademarks in your country and take local advice before you register it.
